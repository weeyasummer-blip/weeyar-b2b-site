VITAMIN SOFTGEL PRODUCT PLANNING

Private Label Vitamin D3 + K2 Sourcing Guide

A practical framework for defining the softgel specification, comparing project routes and requesting information without assuming that every D3 + K2 product is equivalent.

Published September 21, 2026•6-minute read•B2B sourcing guide

Short answer: Compare the exact vitamin identities and forms, declared amounts and serving presentation, carrier oil, softgel shell, complete specification, packaging, sample standard and destination-market requirements before comparing quotations.

1. Turn the product name into a purchasing specification

“Vitamin D3 + K2” identifies a product direction, not a complete specification. Two quotations may refer to different ingredient forms, declared amounts, serving presentations, carrier systems or packaging. Request the specification that applies to the exact option being quoted.

2. Confirm ingredient identity, form and declaration

Ask how each vitamin is identified in the proposed formula and how its amount is presented per unit and per serving. Do not transfer numbers or label language from a reference product to a new project without confirmation.

3. Review the complete softgel system

The carrier oil, softgel shell and other formula components belong to the commercial specification. They can affect dietary positioning, allergen review, stability considerations, labeling and destination-market evaluation.

4. Separate existing, private-label and custom routes

Ask the supplier to identify the route

  • Existing option: review a currently available product and its specification.
  • Private label: use a selected product direction with brand-specific packaging.
  • Custom development: request separate review of formula, amounts, serving or packaging changes.

5. Record what the sample represents

Confirm whether the sample represents current stock, an existing formula in reference packaging or a custom-development version. Record the sample version and confirm which specification would govern production.

6. Confirm packaging before final artwork

Review bottle size, count direction, closure, label area, carton and shipping protection for the selected option. Packaging availability and printed components can affect feasibility and minimum quantities.

7. Match documents to the selected project

Ask which product specifications, quality or test documents and manufacturing-partner information are available for the selected product and facility. Document scope and target-market applicability must be checked rather than assumed.

8. Send a buyer brief that can be quoted

Include these details

  • Destination country and sales channel
  • Existing, private-label or custom route
  • Required formula attributes and serving direction
  • Softgel shell or dietary preferences
  • Bottle, label and carton requirements
  • Estimated quantity and number of variants
  • Samples and documents needed for evaluation

Frequently asked questions

Can an existing softgel use my brand?

Private-label packaging can be discussed for selected options. The available route depends on the chosen product, packaging and quantity.

Should buyers compare only the unit price?

No. Confirm that the quotations refer to the same ingredient forms, declared amounts, serving, softgel system, packaging, documents and commercial route.
